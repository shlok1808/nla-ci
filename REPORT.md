# Independent Research Audit — nla-ci

**TL;DR.** Every headline number reproduces, several to the third decimal. The statistics are clean (all probes held-out, no leakage found, pooled CV is slightly *conservative*). The audit adds five new results that materially shape the paper: (1) **nonlinear probes do not beat the linear probe on leak** — the weak signal is genuinely weak, not nonlinearly hidden; (2) about **a third of the (already weak) leak-vs-not signal is just "absence of deflection"** (cos(v_leak, v_deflect) = −0.52); (3) the leak label is barely recoverable even from the **full response text** (TF-IDF 0.75 ≈ the sweep's activation ceiling), so the leak "ceiling" is partly a label-subtlety ceiling; (4) the position sweep only covers the **first 64 of a median-111-token response** — "never crystallizes" needs that qualifier; (5) the minimal-pairs **lexical confound is near-total (text AUC 0.956)** and inherent, so E3's privileged-delta gate will almost certainly flag — plan the claim language now. One data bug: the L3 tier-4 label fix was never applied to the bf16 CSV (ID 495 is a confirmed judge hallucination, still labeled `leaked`). The strongest paper is the asymmetry/null-as-finding story; the field context (below) supports it and nobody has scooped the verbalization-of-behavior angle.

## 2. Overlooked findings (new results from this audit)

Ordered by how much they should change the paper.

### 2.1 The nonlinear-probe question is already answered — and it's a null (free win)
MLP (1- and 2-layer), RBF-SVM, gradient boosting, and kNN (each on PCA-reduced activations, plus full-dim logistic at two regularizations, 3 seeds each) **all fail to beat the linear probe** on leak: best nonlinear 0.651 vs linear 0.674 (leaked-vs-not); same ordering for leaked-vs-approp (0.639 vs 0.647). The deflection control behaves identically (linear 0.882 ≥ all nonlinear). PCA-rank analysis: leak signal is *low-rank* — 10 PCs give 0.708 (slightly above full-dim, i.e., the rest of the 3584 dims add only noise); deflection saturates at 5 PCs (0.883). **Implication:** "leak is weakly decodable" is not an artifact of linear probing; the information is genuinely thin at this extraction point, and it lives in the top of the variance spectrum, not in some distributed nonlinear code. This closes the "MLP probe on leak" item from the session-09 audit *without a GPU*, and it is exactly the robustness check reviewers of a null ask for. (`scratch/02` A–B.)

### 2.2 A third of the leak-vs-not signal is just "absence of deflection"
cos(diff-means leak direction, diff-means deflection direction) = **−0.52**; leak and deflection probe scores correlate −0.45; erasing the (train-fold) deflection direction drops the leak probe 0.684 → **0.628**. The leaked-vs-not contrast counts refused in "not," so its probe partly reads the (strong) deflection signal in reverse. This *quantifies* why leaked-vs-approp (which drops refused) is lower (0.65) and confirms session 08's L12 recommendation was right — but note the residual leak-specific signal is even weaker than the headline 0.68. Report leaked-vs-approp as canonical and cite this decomposition as the reason. (`scratch/02` C.)

### 2.3 The leak label is barely lexically recoverable even from the full response — the "ceiling" is partly a label-subtlety ceiling
TF-IDF on the **entire response text** recovers the judge's own leak label at only **0.749** (leaked-vs-not; 0.720 vs-approp). The judge produced the label *from that text* — so ~0.75 is roughly what surface lexical evidence supports, and the sweep's activation ceiling (0.747/0.772) sits exactly there. Two readings, both useful: (a) the leak labels hinge on subtle contextual judgments (who is being told what), not on lexical "tells," so *no* bag-of-features probe should be expected to hit 0.9 — this contextualizes the weak activation numbers; (b) mid-response activations carry about as much leak information as a bag-of-words of the transcript, which is what "transcript-borne, not decision-borne" predicts. (`scratch/03`.)

### 2.4 The sweep window covers barely half the response — "never crystallizes" needs a qualifier
100% of tier-3 responses exceed 64 tokens (median 111, max 277). The disclosure the judge cites can occur anywhere, including after k=64. The honest claim is "leak decodability never exceeds 0.77 **within the first 64 response tokens**." Cheap fix on the next Lambda pass: extend the stored positions (or store at relative positions — 10%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 90% of each response) and re-probe. Given §2.3, late crystallization would likely be transcript-reading anyway, but close the hole before a reviewer opens it. (`scratch/03`.)

### 2.5 Free preview of E2: the text-prefix baseline climbs in parallel with the activations
TF-IDF on (scenario + first k response tokens) rises 0.58 → 0.64 over k=0→64 while the best activation AUC moves 0.69 → ~0.74: the activation-over-text increment is roughly **constant (+0.08–0.10) at every position** — there is no position where activations suddenly know something the text doesn't. This is a weak-baseline preview, not a substitute for E2 (a 7B model extracts more from a transcript than TF-IDF does), but it sets the expectation: **E1 − E2 will be modest and flat**, i.e., E2 will most likely *confirm* the "no crystallization" story rather than reveal a hidden decision. Budget accordingly. (`scratch/03`.)

### 2.6 Minimal pairs: length-clean, lexically saturated (quantifies L13 before spending GPU)
The 233 valid pairs are clean on length (Qwen-token Δ mean +1.6, median +1; length-only AUC 0.522 — passes the gate) and cleanly minimal (removing the union of per-pair edit words collapses text AUC to exactly 0.500, so there is no drift outside the edits). But the lexical confound is near-total: **pair-grouped TF-IDF AUC 0.956**; still **0.824** after ablating a 30+-term secrecy/publicity marker lexicon — and the post-ablation discriminative n-grams are *still* secrecy language ("kept," "without," "one who," "it from" vs "knows," "share," "among," "known to"). Function-word-only text separates at 0.915, but that test is contaminated (the strongest markers — "only," "everyone" — *are* stopwords); with markers removed first it is 0.758. **You cannot flip secrecy without flipping its lexical expression — at every level down to prepositions.** Consequences: (i) `stage_validate` check [4] will compare the activation AUC against ≈0.956, so the privileged delta will be ≈0 and the CONFOUND FLAG will fire — decide the claim language *now*, don't discover this at GATE 2; (ii) the honest E3 claim was already written in L13 ("privacy *and its lexical expression* are jointly encoded") — keep it; (iii) see §5/E3 for a validation upgrade (marker-family split) that partially rescues a semantics claim. (`scratch/06`.)

### 2.7 Smaller findings
- **Leak probe transfers across topics but not across task format.** Leave-one-cluster-out (k=5/9 TF-IDF clusters): 0.683/0.670 ≈ within-CV — the weak signal is not topic memorization. Leave-one-info-type-out: 0.660 pooled. But tier-3→tier-4 transfer *fails* (AUC 0.41; 0.32 with ID 495 corrected) — n=20 and a different format (meeting summaries), so exploratory, but do not claim a generalizable "leak direction." Deflection LOTO transfers fine (0.878). (`scratch/02` E–F, `scratch/04`.)
- **Per-info-type heterogeneity is real-looking but statistically unremarkable.** Tier 3 = 9 info types × 30 (block structure verified in file order). Leak rates span 40–77%; per-type AUC of the pooled probe spans 0.47–0.80 — but SD across types (0.10) ≈ the n=30 Hanley SE (0.10). Do not report type-level AUCs as findings; the between-type base-rate component contributes only ~0.025 of the pooled AUC (0.684 pooled vs 0.659 mean-within). (`scratch/04`.)
- **NLA descriptions carry a whiff of response-*style*, zero privacy semantics.** Formal quantification: 100% of descriptions contain format/structure vocabulary (mean 9.3 format terms each; 69% open with "Structured …"); narrow CI lexicon = 21/496 (verifies claim exactly), and a deliberately broad lexicon (trust/reveal/conceal/keep-quiet/…) finds only 21/270 tier-3 hits, balanced across labels (13/151 leaked vs 5/83 appropriate vs 3/36 refused — n.s.). New nuance: k-means clusters of tier-3 descriptions associate weakly with labels (χ² p=0.03) — a "supportive/empathetic" cluster is appropriate-enriched, a "direct-quote" cluster leak/refuse-enriched. So the desc AUCs (0.61 leak / 0.76 refused) ride on verbalized *response-register*, not privacy content. That's a nice mechanistic gloss on "what survives verbalization": tone survives, norms don't. (`scratch/05`.)
- **Judge-noise attenuation, quantified.** With ~10% label error, observed AUC ≈ 0.9·true + 0.05: true deflection ≈ 0.92–0.95, true leak ≈ 0.70. Comparisons unaffected; worth one line in the paper.
- **The implicit-vs-explicit leak split can't be done from existing data** — judge reasoning uses "implicit/allusion" language in only 1/151 leaked rows. If you want it (it would test whether borderline labels dilute the leak signal), it needs a ~$1 re-judge pass with an explicitness field.

## 3. Threats to validity, ranked by kill probability

1. **T1 — "Verbalization destroys the leak signal" is a ~0.03–0.08 effect on the weak axis.** Desc 0.606 vs input 0.581: bootstrap CIs overlap heavily. The *refused* row (desc 0.760 vs input 0.622, acts 0.920) is what actually demonstrates channel loss of privileged content. If the paper leans on the leak row for the "collapse" claim, one competent reviewer kills it. **Fix:** make deflection the demonstration of partial survival, make leak the demonstration that there was little privileged signal to lose (per §2.1 it isn't hidden nonlinearly either), and report paired-bootstrap CIs on the *deltas*, not the raw AUCs.
2. **T2 — Deflection may be generic RLHF caution or eval-awareness, not CI content.** The strongest signal in the paper currently has no content validation. ConfAIde scenarios are conspicuously test-like, and eval-awareness is known to be linearly represented and steerable (Nguyen et al. 2025; and a 2026 critique argues such probes partly track *format*). If the deflection direction is mostly "be careful, this is a test," the CI framing weakens. **Fix:** idea 1 (NLA-verbalize the deflection direction) + a cheap cosine check against an eval-awareness direction derived from contrast prompts + the Patchscopes placebo. This is the highest-value validation gap; it is also cheap (§5/E-NLA, E-EVAL).
3. **T3 — The leak "ceiling" may be a label ceiling.** §2.3: full-response text recovers the label at only 0.75, and judge noise is ~10%. A skeptic can reframe "leak is never decided" as "your leak label is too subtle/noisy to decode." Partial rebuttals already exist (deflection hits 0.89–0.92 under the *same* judge; permutation test confirms the 0.68 is real signal), but the paper needs this stated as a limitation, plus ideally the explicitness re-judge (§2.7). Note the asymmetry survives either way — the honest version is "under an identical labeling pipeline, one behavior is 0.9-decodable pre-response and the other never exceeds 0.77 anywhere we looked."
4. **T4 — E3's lexical confound (§2.6).** Fatal only to a "privacy semantics beyond lexicon" claim, which L13 already forbids. Not fatal to: the norm-vs-behavior dissociation (near-ceiling secret/public encoding vs 0.65 behavior encoding — both probes see the same words, the *contrast* is the point), or the non-circular 2×2 (v_privacy never sees leak labels regardless). Decide claim language before GATE 2; add the marker-family-split validation (§5/E3+).
5. **T5 — Window truncation (§2.4).** Cheap to close; embarrassing if a reviewer finds it first ("your responses are 111 tokens and you probed 64").
6. **T6 — Sweep argmax selection.** 975 correlated AUCs; the 0.7716@k42 "peak" is selected post hoc (Hanley SE ≈ 0.030, neighbor margin ≤1 SE). Session 08 already demoted it; keep it demoted. Note the *null* headline ("no cell ≥0.80") is anti-fragile here — selection bias inflates maxima, so the true ceiling is if anything lower. If any positional claim survives to the paper, validate it split-half across scenarios (needs the Lambda npz, ~10 lines).
7. **T7 — Single model, single benchmark, judge-defined labels, n=36 refused.** Standard scope caveats; the refused-class SE (±0.035) does not threaten the 0.88–0.92 claims. Tier-4 bf16 mislabel (ID 495) must be fixed or tier 4 dropped from every table.

## 4. Scoop risk assessment

- **Wang et al. 2026 (arXiv:2604.00209, "Do LLMs Know What Is Private Internally?") — HIGH conceptual overlap, already published.** They probe CI parameters (info type / recipient / transmission principle) as linearly separable directions, document near-perfect concept probes coexisting with ~40% leak rates, run CI-parametric steering that reduces violations, and *their abstract already claims the headline* "privacy failures arise from misalignment between representation and behavior rather than missing awareness." The repo's planned differentiation (judgment frame vs acting frame; attributes vs behavior) is real but must now carry the whole novelty argument. What they do **not** have: behavior-outcome probes at the moment of action, any positional/crystallization dynamics, any verbalization channel, the deflection/leak dissociation, per-scenario (rather than population-level) encoding-vs-behavior coupling. Lead with those.
- **NLA line (transformer-circuits 2026 + community checkpoints) — LOW direct scoop risk, HIGH field velocity.** The NLA paper's showcase is unverbalized *evaluation awareness* in the Opus 4.6 audit; failure modes documented are confabulation / no mechanistic grounding / excessive expressivity. No application to privacy, refusal, or behavioral-decision directions. **Nobody has verbalized a behavior-predictive direction in a privacy context** — idea 1 (deflection-direction verbalization) appears genuinely open. But the "explain activations in language" method space is crowded and fast: Activation Oracles (arXiv:2512.15674, LatentQA-style general activation QA, matches white-box baselines), Predictive Concept Decoders (arXiv:2512.15712), soft-prompt open-vocabulary explanations (OpenReview x8SVUMo2TL), plus the privileged-information critique (arXiv:2509.13316) which tests Patchscopes/LatentQA/SelfIE on QA/retrieval — none on behavioral self-knowledge. A 3–6-month window is a reasonable planning assumption, not longer.
- **"Linear probes rely on textual evidence" (arXiv:2509.21344) — supports, not scoops.** They show behavior-probe AUROC drops 10–30 points when textual evidence is filtered (sandbagging 0.94→0.57). The triad's input-baseline design is precisely the control this line demands; cite it as independent motivation.
- **Refusal-representation space — crowded, adjacent, citable.** Harmfulness ≠ refusal directions (Zhao et al., arXiv:2507.11878); refusal cones / multiple directions (arXiv:2502.17420, arXiv:2602.02132); token-position refusal-score tracking during generation in reasoning models ("Refusal Falls off a Cliff," arXiv:2510.06036 — closest machinery to the position sweep, different question). CI-preserving *deflection* (in-character topic-steering, zero hard refusals) is a distinct, socially-natural behavior — that's the wedge; say it explicitly and cite all of these.
- **Introspection/eval-awareness (2025–26)** — Lindsey 2025 concept-injection introspection; Needham et al. 2025; Nguyen et al. 2025 probing+steering eval awareness; 2026 critiques (content-agnostic injection detection; format-sensitivity). Not competitors; they are the confound literature for T2 and the framing literature for "models contain decision-relevant state they don't verbalize."
- **Verdict:** the specific intersection — *acting-frame behavior probes × crystallization dynamics × verbalization channel × CI/privacy* — is unclaimed as of this audit. The population-level "knows-but-leaks" gap is taken (Wang). The methodological "verbalizers lose privileged info" question is half-taken (2509.13316, on QA). Move within ~one quarter and lead with the dissociation, not the gap.

## 6. The paper framing

**Recommended title-shaped claim:** *"Deflection is decided, leakage just happens: what an LLM's activations know — and can say — about its own upcoming privacy behavior."*

The single strongest story the verified data supports is the **asymmetry**, told as three levels of a ladder measured under one pipeline:

1. **Norm knowledge (E3): near-ceiling.** The model robustly encodes whether information is secret vs public at the moment of action (expected ≥0.95, jointly with its lexical expression — say so plainly).
2. **Deflection decision: encoded before a word is generated (0.89–0.92), formed L10→L20, peaking exactly at the NLA's layer, flat across the response (front-loaded decision), partially verbalizable (0.76), transfers across topics.**
3. **Leak outcome: never strongly encoded anywhere** — 0.65–0.68 at the decision point, never >0.77 across 5 layers × 65 positions, not nonlinearly hidden (§2.1), a third of it is just "not-deflecting" (§2.2), climbs only as fast as the transcript itself (§2.5), and collapses to the input floor when verbalized. **Leakage is a default, not a decision** — the model never represents "I am about to violate this norm"; it represents "I am not being careful," and the leak follows.

Position against Wang et al. in one sentence: *they showed the norms are known and the behavior is bad (a population-level representation–behavior gap, probed in a judgment frame); we show what is and is not **decided** at the moment of action, and what any language channel can recover of it.* The null results are load-bearing and match current workshop review criteria (falsifiable hypotheses, honest limitations, rigorous negatives welcomed) — do not dress the leak null up as a positive; argue it *as* the finding, with the deflection contrast as the positive control that proves the pipeline could have seen a decision if one existed. If E-NLA (item 7) returns caution/CI language for the deflection direction, that becomes the second figure and the first sentence-level description of a behavioral-decision direction — the NLA contribution the project was originally after, relocated to the signal that can actually support it.
